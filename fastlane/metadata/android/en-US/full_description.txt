SonicRelay streams the audio playing on your computer straight to your phone, with the low latency of a direct peer-to-peer connection.

Start a session on the SonicRelay publisher running on your PC, scan or type the temporary session code on your phone, and listen. Audio travels directly between the two devices over WebRTC — the server only handles sign-in, sessions and signalling, and never relays your audio.

Features

* Low-latency audio over WebRTC, peer-to-peer whenever the network allows.
* Join a session with a short temporary code.
* Live connection monitor: signalling state, ICE state, estimated latency, jitter, and whether the stream is direct or relayed.
* Background playback: keep listening with the screen off or another app in front, with a persistent notification offering Open, Stop and Reconnect.
* Device management: see and revoke the devices registered to your account.
* Dark Material 3 interface.

Bring your own server

SonicRelay is not tied to a single provider. The server address is configurable inside the app, and the backend is free software you can host yourself:

* Backend: https://github.com/vitorhugo-java/dotnet_SonicRelay
* Windows publisher: https://github.com/vitorhugo-java/windows_SonicRelay

You will need a SonicRelay account on whichever server you point the app at, plus the publisher running on the machine whose audio you want to hear.

Privacy

Access tokens and the device identifier are kept in Android's encrypted storage and are never written to logs or plain preferences. The app only receives audio: it never opens your microphone and never sends media of its own.
